01

What a tagged PDF actually is

A PDF page is a sequence of drawing operators. It says "put this glyph at these coordinates in this font." It does not say "this is a level-2 heading." Accessibility in PDF is a parallel structure bolted onto that drawing model, and understanding its four moving parts is the difference between building a real remediation engine and building a wrapper around someone else's API.

  • Marked content. Inside each page's content stream, runs of operators are wrapped in /P <</MCID 0>> BDC … EMC pairs. The MCID is a per-page integer that names that run of ink.
  • The structure tree. /StructTreeRoot in the document catalog holds a tree of StructElem dictionaries — Document → Sect → H1 → P → Table → TR → TH — whose leaves point back at MCIDs. This tree, not the visual layout, is the reading order a screen reader follows.
  • The parent tree. /ParentTree is the reverse index: MCID and annotation → owning structure element. Screen readers use it to answer "what am I inside?" It must stay consistent with the structure tree or the file is broken.
  • The role map and document-level flags. /RoleMap maps custom tags onto the standard set; /MarkInfo <</Marked true>>, /Lang, /ViewerPreferences <</DisplayDocTitle true>> and an XMP dc:title are all separately required.

Why post-hoc tagging is fundamentally lossy

The semantics you are trying to recover existed in the source document and were deliberately thrown away at render time. The CHI 2026 Best Paper iTagPDF (Mowar, Steinfeld & Bigham, CMU) puts it plainly: tags today are generated visually rather than semantically, and when you feed the original source alongside the PDF, the automatically produced metadata exceeds what human authors currently submit to ACM conferences. Their reported figure for the installed base is that roughly 95% of publicly available PDFs remain inaccessible.

Strategic consequence

Any pipeline that only ever looks at the rendered PDF is competing at a permanent information disadvantage. The highest-accuracy path is always source-first: if a DOCX, PPTX, InDesign, HTML or LaTeX original exists, tag from that and export. Build the pixel-and-operator reconstruction path as the fallback, not the flagship. Almost no commercial vendor does this, and it is a genuine differentiator.

02

The compliance target you are aiming at

"WCAG 2.2 compliant PDF" is not one standard. It is the union of two overlapping ones, and conflating them is the single most common source of false compliance claims in this market.

The standards stack, and what each one is for
StandardWhat it isWhat it governs2026 status
PDF/UA-1
Target this		 ISO 14289-1:2014, built on PDF 1.7 File-format requirements: structure tree correctness, artifacts, metadata, annotations, fonts, Unicode mapping. Still the practical target — the version every validator and authoring tool actually supports.
PDF/UA-2 ISO 14289-2:2024, built on PDF 2.0 Adds MathML in the structure tree, expanded annotation rules, new element types. Published; tooling still catching up. Support it as an output option, not the default.

WCAG 2.2 AA
Target this too		 W3C Recommendation, Oct 2023 Outcome requirements: contrast, link purpose, language of parts, meaningful sequence, non-text content. The standard the laws increasingly name. Not satisfiable by tag structure alone.
Matterhorn Protocol 1.1 PDF Association testing model 31 checkpoints, 136 failure conditions that operationalise PDF/UA-1 into testable statements. The de facto QA checklist. Two conditions (23-001, 27-001) have no defined test at all.

The two gaps nobody markets

Gap one: PDF/UA is necessary but not sufficient for WCAG. A file can be flawlessly PDF/UA-1 conformant and still fail WCAG 2.2 AA on 1.4.3 Contrast, 2.4.4 Link Purpose, 3.1.2 Language of Parts, 1.4.1 Use of Colour, and 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast. None of those live in the tag tree. They live in the rendered pixels and in the meaning of the words. Any product promising WCAG 2.2 must run a second, rasterisation-based analysis layer that most PDF tools do not have at all.

Gap two: WCAG 2.2's newest criteria are mostly viewer-level for PDF. 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured, 2.5.7 Dragging, 2.5.8 Target Size, 3.2.6 Consistent Help, 3.3.7 Redundant Entry and 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication are largely inapplicable to a static document — but they do bite on interactive AcroForm PDFs, where field hit-area size and multi-step form flows are yours to answer for. Document that applicability analysis per file rather than hand-waving it; procurement teams increasingly ask.

The machine / human split

Of the Matterhorn Protocol's 136 failure conditions, sources put 87–89 as software-decidable and 47 as requiring human judgment. That ratio — roughly one third of the standard being undecidable by any tool — is the reason Adobe's own community experts state that Acrobat deliberately refuses to stamp the PDF/UA identifier automatically. It is also the number every "100% automated compliance" claim in this market is quietly ignoring.

The conditions that will always need a person

Tags in logical reading order · heading text tagged as a heading and at the right level · irrelevant content correctly marked as Artifact · alternative text that is actually correct and sufficient · OCR text free of significant errors · structure elements nested in a semantically appropriate way · table header/data associations that reflect the real meaning of the table · colour not used as the sole carrier of information.

03

Market timing and regulatory pressure

The demand curve here is regulatory, not aesthetic. Three things drive it, and one of them moved recently in a way that matters for how you sequence the build.

  • ADA Title II (United States). The DOJ web rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA for state and local government content, PDFs explicitly included. On 17 April 2026 the DOJ issued an interim final rule extending the compliance dates by a year: large public entities now have until 26 April 2027, smaller entities and special districts until 26 April 2028. The DOJ has said it fully anticipates enforcing at the new dates. Timing note This bought the market twelve extra months. It did not reduce the backlog — every county, school district and transit authority still has a decade of PDFs to fix, and now has budget cycles in FY27 to do it.
  • European Accessibility Act. Enforceable since 28 June 2025. Fines range from roughly €60,000 (Ireland) to around €900,000 (Sweden). The first EAA lawsuits were filed in France in November 2025 and enforcement is ramping across member states through 2026. EN 301 549 v3.2.1 currently points at WCAG 2.1 AA; v4.1.1, expected during 2026, moves to WCAG 2.2 — so building to 2.2 now is the correct call.
  • Section 508 and sector rules. US federal procurement plus HHS's own May 2026 deadline keep a steady institutional pipeline running independently of Title II.

Scale of the underlying problem

A 2024 study of 20,000 scholarly PDFs published 2014–2023 found that fewer than 3.2% satisfied all six tested accessibility criteria, and 74.9% satisfied none of them. Accessibility actually declined after 2019 among open-access papers. This is not a niche remediation market; it is essentially the entire installed base of PDFs.

The competitive motion to watch: PDFix's Federal Extended POC

PDFix-US is running a three-month pilot for federal and state agencies — roughly 2,500 documents / 25,000 pages for $23,495, pitched directly against "outsourced remediation runs $5–8 per page (about $125,000)." Applications close 31 December 2026, which puts conversions in FY27 exactly as the extended ADA Title II deadline lands. It is a well-timed land-and-expand play and it will win accounts.

Three things in it matter for how you position:

  • The per-page price is a flat fee in disguise. The bundle includes an SDK Enterprise licence with unlimited annual page volume. The advertised ~93¢/page only holds at exactly 25,000 pages; at 100,000 it is 24¢ and at a million it is 2¢. Any high-volume institutional buyer will do this arithmetic, and per-page SaaS loses to it above roughly 30,000 pages a year. Pricing implication See §11 — a flat-rate unlimited tier is not optional.
  • They concede the human-review cost and then exclude it from the quote. The Month 3 deliverable is literally to "measure how long manual review of all 2,500 documents takes", and pricing "excludes internal reviewer labor." At three minutes a page that is ~1,250 hours — $50,000–75,000 of loaded labour, two to three times the software cost, unquoted. That unpriced line item is precisely what the review workspace in §9 exists to attack.
  • It targets WCAG 2.1 AA, not 2.2. Correct for ADA Title II as written today, but EN 301 549 v4.1.1 moves to WCAG 2.2 during 2026. A 2.2-native pitch is a genuine wedge in EU accounts and with anyone future-proofing.

Build vs. buy — and a free move worth making this week

Licensing PDFix SDK Enterprise as your tagging engine would ship months faster than building the writer in §8D. I would still not do it: it makes you a reseller of a direct competitor, puts a floor under your gross margin, and leaves you undifferentiated in the one layer customers can actually see.

But the free PDFix build is fully featured — no key, no registration, all features including auto-tagging — and only watermarks saved files. So install it now and use it as a benchmark opponent on your golden set and as a correctness oracle for what valid PDF/UA output looks like while you debug your own tag writer. Free, legal, and it de-risks the hardest module in the build.

What the evidence collectively says

Structure detection — headings, paragraphs, lists, reading order, simple tables — is now genuinely good: 0.90+ on public benchmarks, and improving fast in open source. Semantic correctness is not. Alt-text quality, complex table header associations, "is this decorative or meaningful", and "does this reading order match the author's intent" all sit in the 0.4–0.7 band for the best available models, with active hallucination risk. A credible product design must treat the first category as automatable and the second as routed, not automated.

06

The loopholes vendors leave open

You asked specifically for this. Here are the ten places where the marketing and the reality diverge, in rough order of how much money is riding on the gap. Each one is simultaneously a competitor weakness and a trap you can fall into yourself.

01

"Compliance score" is a score against a tool, not against a standard

One vendor publishes its formula openly: (Total Checks − Failed Checks) ÷ Total Checks, computed in PAC. PAC only evaluates machine-verifiable conditions. So a document with all 47 human-judgment conditions entirely unexamined — wrong reading order, meaningless alt text, tables whose headers point at the wrong cells — scores 100%. The guarantee attached to that number ("100% Compliance Score Guarantee") is technically satisfiable on a file that a screen reader user cannot use.

Use it Publish a two-number result on every file: machine-verified conformance and human-verified conformance, never a single blended percentage.

02

Stamping the PDF/UA identifier is an assertion, not a computation

The PDF/UA identifier is an XMP entry declaring conformance with ISO 14289. Adobe deliberately does not set it after auto-tagging, on the stated grounds that roughly a third of the criteria require human verification. Any tool that stamps it automatically at the end of an unattended pipeline is making a conformance claim on your behalf that nobody verified.

Use it Gate the identifier behind human sign-off. Make "we don't stamp what we haven't verified" a stated policy — it is a genuine trust differentiator against the $0.30 tier.

03

"PDF/UA compliant" quietly substituted for "WCAG compliant"

The two standards overlap but neither contains the other. Contrast (1.4.3, 1.4.11), link purpose (2.4.4), language of parts (3.1.2), and use of colour (1.4.1) are outside the tag tree entirely. A vendor that validates only with veraPDF or PAC's PDF/UA profile and then sells "WCAG 2.2 AA" has not tested the WCAG-only criteria at all.

Use it Rasterise every page and run real contrast analysis on text runs against their actual backdrops. Almost nobody in the PDF space does this well.

04

Checkers verify that alt text exists, not that it is right

Every automated checker on the market treats a non-empty /Alt string as a pass. Generate "image" for 400 figures and the file passes. Research with real screen reader users found AI descriptions that scored well on technical accuracy still caused measurably worse task completion — and models hallucinate descriptions of images they cannot see in roughly a third of ambiguous cases.

Use it Score alt text on a rubric (identifies subject, conveys function in context, omits redundancy, right length) with a second model and human spot-checks. Never emit placeholder alt text to clear a checker.

05

Complex tables are effectively never automated correctly

Simple tables need TH with a /Scope. Tables with merged or nested headers need every TH to carry an /ID and every TD to carry a /Headers array pointing at the right ones. Practitioner guidance is blunt: auto-tag "almost never creates TH tags correctly." Structure recognition can find the grid; deciding which header governs which cell is semantic work.

Use it Detect complex tables and route them, with a purpose-built grid editor. This is the single highest-value UI you can build.

06

Interactive forms are a separate, mostly unsolved problem

AcroForm fields need tagging, an accessible name (the tooltip), a correct tab order, and correct association with their visible labels. Adobe's own behaviour is that adding a form field does not create the associated tag, so the field is invisible to screen readers. Non-fillable form-like layouts also confuse auto-tag into merging elements. Most "automated remediation" pricing tiers exclude or barely touch forms.

Use it Detect AcroForms at ingest and price them as a distinct product. Every government agency has form PDFs and almost nobody automates them.

07

The carve-outs are buried in the FAQ

Read the fine print and the exclusions appear: scanned pages with unfixable contrast, vector art, CAD drawings, illustrations, mathematical notation, embedded media, third-party subdomains. Adobe states plainly that files containing illustrations, CAD or vector art "may not return high quality results." These carve-outs are rarely surfaced before purchase.

Use it Run a triage pass at upload and show the customer, before they pay, exactly which pages your pipeline can and cannot fully handle.

08

Accuracy percentages with no methodology

"Up to 95% auto-tagging." "95%+ WCAG 2.1 AA." "Near-100% accuracy." None of these are accompanied by a dataset, a per-criterion breakdown, an inter-rater protocol, or a reproducible benchmark. Meanwhile the open-source project that does publish a benchmark reports 0.907 overall and is candid that its own AI "is a strong first opinion, not the last word."

Use it Build a labelled golden set of a few hundred pages across document types and publish per-criterion precision and recall. Nobody in this market has done it. It becomes both your QA harness and your best marketing asset.

09

PAC's own limits are not the same as PDF/UA's limits

PAC 2024's technical tests are based on WCAG 2.1, not 2.2. It flags reading order and contrast as visual-inspection warnings rather than hard failures. Acrobat's checker and PAC disagree routinely, and passing one does not imply passing the other. A pipeline tuned to make PAC go green is optimising for a proxy.

Use it Validate against multiple independent engines (veraPDF profiles, PAC, Acrobat) and report disagreement as a signal rather than hiding it.

10

The FTC has already priced this kind of claim

In January 2025 the FTC ordered accessiBe to pay $1 million for deceptive claims that its automated product made websites WCAG compliant; the final order was approved in April 2025. Two findings are directly on point here: the claims "were not supported by competent and reliable evidence," and the company failed to adequately disclose that certain content types — PDFs explicitly named — were not remediated unless separately purchased. The FTC also cited undisclosed material connections to seemingly independent reviewers.

Use it Treat substantiation as a build requirement, not a legal afterthought. See §10.

07

Open-source bill of materials

The single most important finding of this research: you no longer have to build or license a tagger. As of Q2 2026 there is an Apache-2.0 engine, built with the PDF Association and the veraPDF developers, that takes an untagged PDF and emits a Tagged PDF, and it currently leads the public extraction benchmarks. What it does not give away is the final PDF/UA export step. That is the seam to build in.

08

Recommended architecture

One non-negotiable design constraint drives everything: do not rebuild the PDF. These are legal, financial and regulatory documents. Regenerating them from extracted content changes pagination, kerning, signature appearance and print output, and destroys the customer's trust the first time a figure moves. Every fix must be an in-place object-graph edit that leaves the rendered pixels byte-identical.

A · INGEST

Triage and route before you spend a cent

Classify each page: digital-born text / scanned raster / vector-heavy / form / mathematical / mixed. Detect an existing structure tree and its quality. Ask whether a source document exists. Produce a per-page confidence forecast and a quotable price before processing, with explicit "cannot fully automate" flags. This single step neutralises loophole #7 and turns your carve-outs into a trust signal.

Source-first path, when the original exists

If the customer can supply the DOCX, PPTX, InDesign, HTML or LaTeX original, remediate there and export a tagged PDF. This is the highest-accuracy path available and it is the direct implication of the iTagPDF result. It also produces a durable fix: next month's version of the document comes out accessible by default.

Word/LibreOffice export · InDesign export · HTML→tagged PDF · tagpdf (LaTeX)
C · LAYOUT

Structure recovery

Deterministic engine first: OpenDataLoader local mode at 0.015 s/page gives reading order, headings, lists, simple tables and bounding boxes, with reproducible output. Escalate only the pages it flags as uncertain to the hybrid AI mode or to Docling/TableFormer. Deterministic-by-default matters: customers re-running the same file must get the same result, and you must be able to explain why a tag was chosen.

OpenDataLoader (XY-Cut++) → Docling/TableFormer → PaddleOCR/Tesseract for scans
D · WRITE

The tag writer — the module you own

Inject BDC/EMC pairs with fresh MCIDs into each page's content stream without altering any drawing operator; build the StructTreeRoot hierarchy; maintain /ParentTree consistency; write /RoleMap, /MarkInfo, /Lang, /ViewerPreferences and XMP dc:title; artifact headers, footers, watermarks and decorative rules; associate link annotations with Link structure elements; attach MathML as an associated file on Formula elements.

Verify byte-level render equivalence after every write by rasterising before and after and comparing.

pikepdf / qpdf · custom Python module · render-diff gate
E · ENRICH

The semantic layer, confidence-gated

Alt text and figure descriptions, table header/scope/ID assignment, list normalisation, decorative-vs-meaningful decisions, link purpose rewriting, language-of-parts detection, formula MathML. Run a cheap model over every page, escalate low-confidence pages to a frontier model, and escalate disagreement between the two to a human. Never write a value the model was not confident about — leave it empty and flag it, because an empty alt attribute that a human fills is far better than a plausible fabrication that clears a checker.

Claude Haiku 4.5 first pass → Sonnet 5 / Opus 5 escalation → Batch API for bulk → prompt caching on the shared rubric
F · VERIFY

Three-layer verification

See §9. This is the product.

G · CERTIFY

Evidence pack, not a green tick

Emit a versioned conformance report: which profile was run, which rules passed, which human conditions were verified, by whom, when, with what evidence, and what remains unverified. Only stamp the PDF/UA identifier when a named human has signed the human-judgment checklist. Generate an accessibility conformance report (ACR/VPAT-style) suitable for procurement.

Deployment notes for your existing stack

The site is Next.js 16 on Vercel with Clerk, Drizzle/Postgres, Supabase and both Stripe and Razorpay already wired. Keep the app there and put the pipeline behind a queue, because PDF work is minutes-long and memory-hungry, not request-shaped.

  • Worker tier: a containerised Python + Java service (OpenDataLoader needs a JVM) on Fly.io, Railway or AWS ECS Fargate. Not Vercel functions.

  • Reuse what exists: app/tools/pdf-accessibility-checker and app/tools/alt-text-generator are already live and already have the pdf-lib / pdfjs-dist plumbing. The checker is the natural free on-ramp to the paid pipeline.

Designing the verification, not the tagger

Everything above this section is table stakes within eighteen months. This section is the durable product.

Layer 1 — machine, deterministic

Run veraPDF's published profiles and turn the raw output into findings against your own model. Measured rule counts in the current profile repository:

veraPDF validation profiles — rule counts as of August 2026
ProfileRulesUse it for
PDFUA-1.xml106The core machine-decidable PDF/UA-1 conformance gate. Non-negotiable pass condition.
PDFUA-2.xml91PDF/UA-2 output option for customers on PDF 2.0.
WCAG-2-2-Machine.xml106Machine-decidable WCAG 2.2 rules layered on PDF/UA-1.
WCAG-2-2-Complete.xml152Adds semantic heuristics — see below. Your independent second opinion.
WTPDF-1-0-Accessibility.xml1,723Exhaustive parent/child structure relationship validation. Catches malformed trees your writer produces.

The trick worth stealing

veraPDF's WCAG-2-2-Complete profile contains heuristic semantic rules, not just format rules. Real rule descriptions from the shipped profile: "This structure element should be a heading instead of a paragraph", "The number of columns of this table does not match the number of columns of the visual representation of this table", "This cell is to the right of some cells in the next column", "This structure element should be a table cell instead of a table header", "Redundant heading", "TOCI page label shall match the destination page".

Run these against your own tagger's output. Your tagger and this verifier are independent codebases written by different people from different specifications — so agreement is real evidence, and disagreement is a precise, actionable routing signal for human review. No competitor is using it this way.

Layer 1b — the WCAG-only checks nobody runs

Rasterise each page and compute genuine contrast ratios for every text run against the pixels actually behind it (1.4.3, 1.4.6), plus non-text contrast for UI-like graphics (1.4.11). Detect colour-only information encoding (1.4.1). Evaluate link text for purpose in isolation (2.4.4/2.4.9). Detect language changes mid-document (3.1.2). This is where "WCAG 2.2" stops being a marketing word.

Layer 2 — AI, adversarial and confidence-scored

Not a second tagger — a reviewer. Give a vision model the rendered page and the tag tree side by side and ask it to find disagreements between what is drawn and what is declared. Score alt text against a published rubric. Judge whether table headers match the table's meaning. Critically, calibrate for the failure modes the ASSETS '25 benchmark found: require the model to emit Not Present and Cannot Tell as first-class answers, and treat a description of an image the model was not given as an automatic escalation.

Layer 3 — human, structured and evidenced

A review workspace, not a tag tree in a sidebar. Ordered roughly by how much review time each saves:

  1. Reading-order overlay — numbered boxes drawn over the rendered page, drag to reorder, keyboard-operable.
  2. Table grid editor — the merged-header case made tractable: click a header, paint the cells it governs, generate /ID and /Headers automatically.
  3. Alt-text queue — image, surrounding context, AI draft, rubric score, accept/edit/mark-decorative, batched across the whole document.
  4. Heading outline — the document's H-structure as a tree, with level-skip warnings inline.
  5. Screen reader preview — linearised text as an AT would encounter it, with artifacts hidden.
  6. The 47-condition checklist — each human-judgment Matterhorn condition as an explicit item, pre-answered where Layer 2 was confident, requiring an affirmative human tick where it was not, capturing who and when.

Real screen-reader verification (NVDA or JAWS against Acrobat) resists automation and should be a sampled activity with recorded evidence, not a claimed per-file step. Say so.

Target: get a straightforward 20-page report from raw upload to signed-off in under fifteen minutes of human time. Against a manual baseline of several hours, that is the entire economic argument for the product.

Claims policy and legal exposure

Given the FTC's $1M accessiBe order, the claims policy is a build requirement with the same status as the schema. Write it before you write the landing page.

Never say

  • "AI makes your PDFs WCAG compliant" — the exact claim the FTC found deceptive.
  • "100% compliant" on any unattended automated output.
  • "Certified" for anything a named human did not sign.
  • Any accuracy percentage you cannot reproduce from a published dataset.
  • "Guaranteed to pass an audit" — you do not control the auditor.

Say instead

  • "Passes all 106 machine-verifiable PDF/UA-1 rules and all 106 machine-verifiable WCAG 2.2 rules in veraPDF, validated on 21 Aug 2026."
  • "All 47 Matterhorn conditions requiring human judgment were reviewed and signed off by [name] on [date]."
  • "3 items could not be automatically remediated and are listed with recommended manual fixes."
  • "Automated remediation reduces manual effort; it does not replace human verification."

Two SKUs, one honest boundary

Sell Remediated (machine-verified) and Certified (human-verified) as separate products at separate prices. Only the second stamps the PDF/UA identifier, only the second issues a conformance statement, and the first says clearly on its own output what has and has not been verified. This maps exactly onto the real machine/human split in the standard, which means the boundary is defensible rather than arbitrary.

Substantiation file

Maintain a dated, versioned record of your golden-set benchmark results, methodology, and the per-criterion numbers behind every public claim. The FTC's finding against accessiBe was not "the product is bad" — it was "the claims were not supported by competent and reliable evidence." A benchmark you can hand a regulator is the difference.

Patent exposure — get counsel before launch

US 11,675,970 B2 and its continuation US 12,118,294, assigned to Open Text Corporation, priority date 14 February 2020, expiring 2041, claim a method that: collects training data from structured PDFs, trains a machine learning engine on it, and uses that engine to convert untagged PDFs into tagged, accessible PDFs. That is a broad reading of the mainstream approach.

This is not legal advice and I am not qualified to give it. Get a freedom-to-operate opinion. Note for that conversation that potential design-arounds exist: deterministic geometric analysis rather than a trained classifier (OpenDataLoader's local mode), general-purpose vision-language models not trained on a tagged-PDF corpus, and source-first tagging that never classifies a rendered page at all.

Data handling

These documents are often confidential — medical records, court filings, tender responses, financial statements. Commit publicly and contractually to: no training on customer content, encryption in transit and at rest, a stated retention window with hard deletion, named subprocessors, and a data residency option. Competitors already advertise US-only processing; if you are serving EU customers under the EAA, an EU processing region will become a sales requirement.

Build plan, effort and unit economics

Sequenced so that each phase ships something sellable and each phase's output de-risks the next.

Phase 0 — Turn the free checker into a real one

2–3 weeks
  • Replace the current heuristic checker at app/tools/pdf-accessibility-checker with a veraPDF-backed worker running the PDFUA-1 and WCAG-2-2-Machine profiles.
  • Report findings in three buckets: machine-passed, machine-failed, requires-human-judgment — with the 47 conditions named explicitly.
  • Add the rasterised contrast pass, since nobody else's free checker does it.
  • Why first: it is a genuine free tool that ranks, it educates the market on the machine/human split you will later charge for, and every upload is a qualified lead with a page count attached.

Phase 1 — Auto-tag MVP, digital-born text PDFs only

6–8 weeks
  • Worker service: OpenDataLoader local mode → your pikepdf-based PDF/UA finisher → veraPDF gate → render-diff gate.
  • Scope discipline: no scans, no forms, no complex tables, no maths. Triage rejects them with a clear explanation. Shipping a narrow thing that works beats a broad thing that half-works.
  • Alt text with Haiku-first / Sonnet-escalation, rubric-scored, left empty and flagged when confidence is low.
  • Build the golden set in parallel — 200–300 pages, hand-labelled, spanning report / brochure / academic / government-form / scan. This is your regression harness forever.

Phase 2 — The review workspace

6–8 weeks
  • Reading-order overlay, table grid editor, alt-text queue, heading outline, screen reader preview, 47-condition checklist.
  • Full keyboard operability and screen reader support in the workspace itself — an inaccessible accessibility tool is an own goal you will be called out for.
  • This is where the price moves from $0.50 to $2–3 per page, because it is where verified output becomes possible.

Phase 3 — The hard content types

6–8 weeks
  • Scanned documents: OCR with quality gating, because OCR error rate is itself a conformance condition.
  • Complex tables: automated /ID and /Headers generation from the grid editor's paint interaction.
  • AcroForms: field tagging, tooltips as accessible names, tab order, label association. Priced separately.
  • Formulas: MathML as an associated file on Formula elements, with alt-text fallback.

Phase 4 — Certification and evidence

4 weeks
  • Signed conformance reports, ACR/VPAT-style output, public per-document verification URLs.
  • Human sign-off workflow gating the PDF/UA identifier.
  • Publish the golden-set benchmark. This is the marketing asset and the substantiation file simultaneously.

Phase 5 — Scale and moat

Ongoing
  • Batch API, watched folders, CMS connectors, SharePoint/Drive sync, on-prem deployment for government.
  • Source-first connectors — Word add-in, InDesign export, LaTeX pipeline. Highest accuracy, highest switching cost, and almost no competitor has it.
  • Extend to DOCX/PPTX/XLSX remediation, which is where the recurring revenue is.

Suggested pricing

Free: checker, unlimited, no remediation — the lead magnet. $0.75/page: Remediated, machine-verified, no human sign-off, no PDF/UA stamp. $2.50/page: Certified, human-verified, PDF/UA stamped, conformance report included. You undercut the $12 human tier by 5× while holding a much better gross margin than the $0.30 race-to-zero tier, and the two SKUs mirror the standard's own machine/human boundary.

Per-page pricing has a hard ceiling — you need a flat tier

PDFix sells federal agencies an SDK Enterprise licence with unlimited annual page volume inside a $23,495 bundle (§4). Run the comparison: at 25,000 pages a year, $0.75/page is $18,750 and competitive. At 100,000 pages it is $75,000 against their $23,495. At 500,000 pages it is $375,000 against the same $23,495. Metered pricing loses every large institutional account above roughly 30,000 pages a year, and those are exactly the accounts with a decade of PDFs and an April 2027 deadline.

Fix it with a third SKU: Platform — flat annual fee, unlimited pages, on-prem or private-region deployment, SSO, audit logs, source-first connectors, and a named verification lead. Price it against the labour it replaces, not the pages it processes. Their own POC hands you the argument — their quote excludes $50,000–75,000 of reviewer time, and your workspace is what compresses it. Sell "software plus the review hours it removes", and the flat number can be several times theirs while still being the cheaper line in the agency's budget.

What I would build

Not an AI auto-tagger. That race is over and Apache-2.0 won it. Build the thing that the auto-taggers all explicitly refuse to do: assert, with evidence, that a specific document conforms.

The shape of it: upload a PDF, get a per-page triage and a quote in seconds; automated remediation lands a machine-verified file in minutes; a purpose-built workspace takes a human through only the decisions a machine genuinely cannot make; and the output is a stamped, signed, permanently verifiable conformance record you can hand to a procurement officer, a regulator, or a plaintiff's lawyer.

Four things make that defensible, and none of them are the model:

  1. The verification layer — independent engines cross-checking your own output, including veraPDF's semantic heuristics used adversarially and a rasterised contrast pass almost nobody runs.
  2. The review workspace — compressing hours of expert work into minutes is the actual product, and it is a real UI engineering effort rather than a prompt.
  3. The published benchmark — per-criterion precision and recall on a labelled golden set. Every competitor is quoting unsubstantiated percentages; being the only one with numbers is both the marketing and the FTC defence.
  4. Source-first remediation — the CHI 2026 result says this beats everything else, and essentially nobody is productising it.

It also fits the business already standing. The site has an audit practice, a remediation-support service, compliance documentation, procurement pages and a live PDF checker with the PDF plumbing already installed. A verification-first PDF platform is not a pivot into an unrelated market — it is the same customer, the same standard, the same buyer, with a product attached to the service.

The one-sentence positioning

Everyone else automates the tagging and hopes. We automate the tagging, verify it against the standard with independent engines, route only what genuinely needs a human, and give you the evidence.
